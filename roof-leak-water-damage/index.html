Roof Leak Water Damage in Fort Collins: What to Know

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Catch the DripsFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Lay towels or plastic sheeting down to protect the floor.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofDo not climb onto a wet roof or crawl through a dark attic. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.
  • Corroded or loose metal flashing where the roof meets a chimney, a vent or a wall.
  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
  • A fallen limb or large hail that punctures or bruises the roof covering.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Attic Insulation

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

Mold in the Attic

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours and spread across the underside of the decking.

Inside the Walls

Water that reaches the top of a wall runs down inside it, wetting insulation and the back of the drywall. Bubbling paint or a swollen baseboard may be the only sign.

Lights and Wiring

Leak water heads for openings in the ceiling, such as recessed lights, fan boxes and junction boxes. Moisture in a fixture can cause a short and later corrosion, which is why it needs inspection before use.
