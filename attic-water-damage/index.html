Attic Water Damage in Fort Collins

Attic water damage comes from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Fort Collins inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest
  • Water droplets or rust on the tips of nails that poke through the roof sheathing

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Ceiling Collapse

Saturated insulation is heavy. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

Wet Wiring

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. Water in or on them is a shock and fire risk, so stay out until the power is checked.

Air Conditioner Drips

In a lot of homes the attic houses an air handler along with its condensate line and pan. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Framing

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Correct the Source

We point out the roof or ventilation problem that needs correcting and can share our findings with your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
