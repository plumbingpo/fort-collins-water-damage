Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in Fort Collins

Hardwood responds fast when it gets wet. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

Signs to Look For

  • Earthy odor at floor level even though the surface looks clean
  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
  • Dark gray or black stains along seams and around nail heads
  • Planks with a rounded hump down the center and lower sides
  • Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot or flex more than before
  • Fasteners working loose, heard as squeaks where the floor was soaked
What We Do

How We Handle Hardwood Floor Water Damage

1

Measure the Moisture

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Remove Surface Water

We extract any standing water immediately and clear furniture and rugs from the wet section. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Track Progress and Report

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Water That Keeps Moving

Moisture beneath flooring does not stay put. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.

Sanding Too Early

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

Moisture Under the Boards

Long after the boards seem dry, the subfloor may still be holding water. Unless that layer is dried as well, expect renewed cupping or mold on the underside.

Mold Under Boards

Between the boards and the subfloor there is only darkness and still air. Mold in that layer cannot be seen from the room, and a musty odor is usually the first sign.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Goes Under

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. The surface may wipe dry while the underside stays soaked.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. You might notice haze in the topcoat. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Pressure Builds Up

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.
