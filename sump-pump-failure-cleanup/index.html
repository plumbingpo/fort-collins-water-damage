Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Fort Collins

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • The float getting caught on the side of the basin or tangled in its cord.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Outlets and Wiring

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Boxes and Stored Items

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Basement Floor Coverings

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

How the Quote Is Built

  • How many stored belongings have to be moved, cleaned or thrown away.
  • Whether a temporary pump is needed to keep groundwater down during the work.
  • The time that passed before the water was pumped out and drying started.
  • The depth of the water and the share of the basement floor under it.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Clean and Sanitize

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Confirm

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.
