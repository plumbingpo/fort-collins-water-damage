Crawl Space Water Removal: What Fort Collins Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

Weakened Floor Framing

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.

The answer turns on where the water came from. A sudden, accidental event such as a burst supply pipe beneath the floor is frequently covered. Seepage from the ground, surface floods and long running slow leaks typically fall outside a regular policy. Read your policy or ask your agent, and our team will supply photos and moisture readings to support a claim.
Removing the standing water is quick, generally a matter of a few hours. Drying the framing takes longer, typically 3 to 5 days with a dehumidifier and air movers running under the house. It can run longer when the beams are large, the ground is uncovered, or the flooding went unnoticed for weeks. The job ends when the wood tests dry, whatever the date says.
For a little clean water, a shop vac is fine. Joists and subfloor stay wet, though, since a box fan only pushes moist air in circles. Keep in mind the hazards too, such as little room, no light, and maybe energized wiring or sewage contamination. Professional pumping and drying is the safer choice once the water is more than a thin puddle.
Usually it does need to go. Fiberglass batts hold water like a sponge, lose their insulating value and keep the subfloor above them wet. Heavy with water, they eventually sag and come down. Taking out what is wet allows the lumber to dry, and replacement insulation follows when moisture readings return to normal.
Moist ground after a downpour is nothing unusual, though actual puddles are not something to leave alone. Even an inch or two keeps the humidity high enough to rot wood and grow mold. If water is still there a day after the rain stops, or shows up in dry weather, have it checked.
