Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. Repair is a different job. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Fort Collins crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. Each is its own phase of the job. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. First is source control, which keeps more water from coming in. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Containment, using plastic barriers and controlled airflow so moisture and contaminants stay out of clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

What to Expect From Our Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. Should a lasting fix require a plumber or a roofer, our team coordinates when that happens.

2

Remove Standing Water

We extract standing water using pumps and extraction units, then check carpet and its padding. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Set Up Containment

Plastic barriers and controlled airflow separate wet rooms from dry ones. As a result, contaminants, dust and humid air stay put instead of spreading around the house mid-job.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Each visit includes logged moisture readings, and the structure is usually ready for repair work within 3 to 5 days.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Water is moving from one room into the next along the flooring.
  • Underfoot the carpet is spongy because the padding below holds water.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • Since the leak you have noticed puffed-up baseboards or gaps opening behind them.
  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.
  • Slope the soil away from the house so rainwater does not pool against the walls.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
  • An automatic valve on the main line can cut the water as soon as it detects a leak.
  • In cold months, a heated house protects pipes from freezing, even while you travel.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Mitigation

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Has the water shifted category since the leak started, and is it Category 1, 2 or 3 now? Is removing the cabinet toe kick necessary? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Owners are required by most policies to take reasonable steps against further damage, and the insurer will ask for evidence. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. The result is a file documenting both the damage and the steps taken. It also gives the repair contractor a clear starting point, with no wet surprises behind new drywall.
